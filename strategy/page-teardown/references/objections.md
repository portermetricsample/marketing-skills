# Objection types

The reasons someone doesn't act. A page converts by **answering the ones live for its audience** — not all of them, every time. In a teardown, map each objection to *where* the page answers it (a section, a comparison, a proof point, a line) and flag the ones it **leaves live**. Match objection to awareness: most-aware readers raise Price/Contractual; unaware readers raise Need/Status quo.

| Objection | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Price** | It costs too much |
| **Trust** | I don't believe it works |
| **Effort** | It looks like too much work |
| **Fit** | It's not for a situation like mine |
| **Timing** | Not now |
| **Switching** | I already have something |
| **Need** | I don't have the problem you're solving |
| **Authority** | I like it, but I can't approve the purchase |
| **Alignment** | My team can't agree on the decision |
| **Status quo** | I know we have a problem, but doing nothing is easier |
| **Contractual** | I won't sign these legal terms |
| **Source** | I've heard bad things, or I doubt your company will last |
| **Risk** | What could go wrong, and what am I giving access to |
| **Compliance** | This doesn't meet my legal or security requirements |

> **Evidence answers objections.** The number, name or artifact that proves a claim is what turns a Trust or Need objection. No evidence → the objection stands.

**An unanswered live objection is a leak.** In the value read, a `weak`/`partial` on **Completeness** almost always traces to an objection the page never addressed (often Price or Switching).
