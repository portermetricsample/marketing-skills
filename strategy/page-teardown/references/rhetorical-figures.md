# Rhetorical figures

The figures that show up in page copy. In a teardown, name the ones the page actually uses and quote the line. Not an exhaustive rhetoric course — the ~20 that earn their keep in headlines, subheads, and CTAs, grouped by what they do.

## Repetition & rhythm
| Figure | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Anaphora** | Same words open consecutive lines | "No bosses. No hours. No ceiling." |
| **Epistrophe** | Same words end consecutive lines | "If you don't measure, you lose. If you don't optimize, you lose." |
| **Tricolon** | Three parallel members — the rule of three | "Connect it, see it, decide." |
| **Parallelism** | Different content on the same sentence template | "Hiring adds capacity. Documenting adds capacity. Only one adds margin." |
| **Asyndeton** | Short clauses, no conjunctions — speed | "Sign. Onboard. Bill. Repeat." |

## Progression
| Figure | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Gradatio (climax)** | A ladder — each step builds on the last | "Grow from zero to one to one million." |
| **Anadiplosis** | End one clause, open the next with that word | "Traffic brings clicks, clicks bring sales, sales bring time." |

## Contrast
| Figure | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Antithesis** | Two opposed ideas in one line | "They grow by hiring. You grow by cutting." |
| **Correctio** | Correct yourself mid-line to raise the stakes | "It's not cheap. It's the cheapest thing you'll buy." *(Note: the "not X, it's Y" variant is heavily overused — flag it when a page leans on it.)* |
| **Oxymoron** | Two opposite words joined | "The deafening silence of an unhappy customer." |
| **Chiasmus** | Two halves in mirror; the second inverts the first | "Made for marketers, by marketers." |

## Figurative
| Figure | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Metaphor** | Name one thing by another it resembles | "Your vendor isn't a vendor. It's a second payroll." |
| **Personification** | A thing acts like a person | "Buffer will help you triage comments." |
| **Hyperbole** | Open exaggeration | "The most complete set of integrations, ever." |
| **Metonymy** | Name a thing by something attached to it | "The office decided; payroll paid." |

## Question forms
| Figure | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Rhetorical question** | A question whose answer is obvious | "How many hours a week go to work a system could do?" *(the most overused figure online — only lands if the obvious answer stings)* |
| **Hypophora** | Ask the reader's question, answer it at once | "Is it cheaper? No. Does it save you hiring? Yes." |

## Sound & sentence
| Figure | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Alliteration** | Same opening sound across words | "Measure, improve, multiply." |
| **Aphorism** | A general truth in its shortest, quotable form | "Systems scale; effort doesn't." |

**How to use it in a teardown:** most pages carry a couple of figures deliberately (a gradatio in the hero, a tricolon in the subhead) and a few by habit. Name them, quote them, and flag any that are *overused* patterns (correctio's "not X, it's Y"; the bare rhetorical question) — they signal formula rather than craft.
